Researched 2026-11-07. Confidence: **H** high (primary source read), **M** medium (secondary source, or inferred from a primary source), **L** low (indirect or unconfirmed). "Not found" means I could not confirm it, not that it does not exist.

Method and limits: the session's shared web-search budget ran out after two searches. Everything else comes from fetching known URLs directly, querying the official MCP Registry API, querying the GitHub repository search API, and reading this repo's `research/` files. ## Implications for OpenInvestorPortal

- **Direct competitor (closed, custodial): Canoe, now Bloomberg.** It collects from 3,000+ portals by logging in for clients with APIs or RPA, and is being wired into Bloomberg PORT and ASKB. It is the incumbent "one connection to every GP portal". OIPP's no-custody, vendor-endorsed model is the clearest point of difference, and Canoe is also the scraper vendors would most like to replace.
- **Other custodial aggregators that compete for the same LP budget: Arch, Alkymi, Carta LP Portfolio Analytics (ex-Accelex), iCapital (with AltExchange).** Treat them as potential **OIPP clients**: an OIPP endpoint lets them drop RPA for a portal. Do not pitch them as partners for the registry.
- **Closest analog in form: wealthtech.dev.** It is an open (CC BY 4.0), sourced registry of wealth-tech access with REST, MCP and llms.txt, but it has zero private-markets coverage. It is a partner, not a competitor: cross-link, keep compatible field names, and consider a data exchange. Note the licence difference: CC0 here, CC BY there.
- **No open LP-portal registry, unified LP-portal API or "open banking for private markets" protocol was found** on the web pages checked or on GitHub. The gap the README claims appears real (M).
- **Addepar now has a verified MCP server with private-fund capital-activity tools** (Aug 2026). The registry entry `addepar-client-portal.json` still says `mcp: none` and should be corrected. Addepar, PitchBook and Datasite are now the main "Claude-native" reference points for vendors.
- **Payload alignment: build OIPP schemas from the 2025 ILPA set.** Use Capital Call & Distribution v2.0 for `capital-calls` and `distributions`, Reporting Template v2.0 for `capital-account-statements`, and Performance v1.1 with a granular or gross-up flag. ILPA publishes no machine-readable schema, so a JSON Schema of the ILPA definitions, offered to ILPA, is a credible standards contribution and a partnership opening.
- **Secondary alignment for European coverage:** INREV SDDS fields for real-estate funds, FundsXML for holdings and NAV exports to DACH systems, EDCI metrics as an optional ESG document type, and Open Protocol for hedge-fund portals. BVI formats are unconfirmed and worth a direct inquiry.
- **Transport:** keep RFC 9421. Borrow consent and OAuth patterns from FDX and Akoya ("credentials aren't copied or stored") so the pitch reads as the private-markets version of US open finance.
- **Regulation as tailwind:** the FiDA proposal names AIFMs and UCITS ManCos as data holders and covers investments and real estate. If it advances, EU GPs will need a sharing scheme, and OIPP should be ready to propose itself as one. Its status needs checking.
- **Regulation as cost:** DORA makes Managed Portal Vaults and Gateway Mode B an ICT third-party service for EU AIFMs and LP financial entities (contract terms, register of information, exit plans). Keep Mode A free of custody and out of the data path, and budget DORA-ready contracts for the paid tier.
